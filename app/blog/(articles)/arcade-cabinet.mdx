In 2020, at the Northern Institute of Technology (St Leonards), I saw an arcade machine that had been hacked to run DOOM using an x86 PC.

It was quite janky, but it was so incredibly cool I had to build one.

A few months later, a construction site near me house threw some wood away into a skip. Since it was in the bin I took the wood and drew a frame for an arcade machine.

<center>
  <img src="/arcade/1.jpg" width="60%"/>
</center>

<br />

I bought some ply wood and with a jigsaw cut the shape out. I flashed a raspberry pi 4b with Lakka OS and had some emulators running.

<center>
  <img src="/arcade/2.jpg" width="60%"/>
</center>

<br />

It was almost complete structually.

<center>
  <img src="/arcade/3.jpg" width="60%"/>
</center>

<br />

But I made one fatal mistake. There's a panel where the buttons exist, I wanted to make it extra strong so people could really whack the buttons without bending the frame.

<center>
  <img src="/arcade/4.jpg" width="60%"/>
</center>

<br />

I accidentally made it too thick, and I glued and nailed it into place and couldn't fix it with the tools I had on hand. 

So I threw the cabinet into the trash and thought I'd start again.

I had some wood left over so I made a work bench and a bookshelf too.

That was 5 years ago.

I finally got around to making it again. I was at Robots and Dinosaurs in Meadowbank and found some e-waste. It was a dead x86 machine with a corrupted drive. They also had about 10 monitors stiting around so I took one and went to fixing the mini-pc.

<center>
  <img src="/arcade/8.png" width="90%"/>
</center>

<br />

I flashed Batocera OS onto the mini-pc, fixed it, and had it running gaming emulators.

This time, being more experienced, I moddeled it in CAD using Fusion rather than free-handing it.

<center>
  <img src="/arcade/9.png" width="90%"/>
</center>

<br />

I then bought some wood from bunnings, totalling almost $250 AUD and used the laser cutter at R&D to cut it.

<center>
  <img src="/arcade/10.png" width="60%"/>
</center>

<br />

Assembly took about a day. Starting at 11am it was finished by about 5pm.

<center>
  <img src="/arcade/11.png" width="60%"/>
</center>

<br />

Over all, it took about a day to fix the PC, a day and a half to model it in CAD, and a day to put it together. Around 3 days total, planning and executing.

Everyone loved it, it was a great success. I had a lot of suggestions and support from [Mitch Pommers](https://www.pommers.org), so thank you to him for saving me from my own mistakes a few times.
